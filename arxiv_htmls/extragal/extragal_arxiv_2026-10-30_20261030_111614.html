PRISMS GHZ8: Evidence for a Young Burst and Potential Merger Activity at z=10.23

Published: 2026-10-29

Abstract: UV-bright galaxies in the early Universe provide important constraints on the rapid build-up of stellar mass and the sources responsible for cosmic reionization. However, the physical processes that drive their high UV luminosities remain unclear. Here we report JWST/MIRI LRS spectroscopy of GHZ8, a UV-bright galaxy at z=10.23 with a magnification $μ=1.23$. The spectrum shows a $\sim 5σ$ detection for the $\rm Hβ$-[OIII] complex and $\sim 4σ$ for $\rm Hα$ line, with fluxes of $3.2\pm0.9\times10^{-18}$ and $\rm 1.7\pm0.4\times10^{-18}\,erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}$, respectively. Based on JWST/NIRCam imaging, we find that GHZ8 consists of two components separated only by 0.16" ($\approx600$ pc): a bright, extended main component and a faint, compact southeastern clump, hereafter referred to as GHZ8-main and GHZ8-SE. Through morphological decomposition, we find demagnified $M_{UV}=-19.91\pm0.10$ and $-18.03\pm0.15$ mag for GHZ8-main and GHZ8-SE, with blue UV slopes of $β_{UV}=-2.53\pm0.22$ and $-3.45\pm0.49$, respectively, indicating that both components host young stellar populations. In particular, the extremely blue UV continuum of GHZ8-SE requires an extremely young, ISM-poor stellar population with a high ionizing photon escape fraction, $f_{esc}= 0.6-1.0$. Combining the UV and $\rm Hα$ luminosities with the results from the spectro-photometric SED fitting, we constrain the star-formation activity of GHZ8-main, and find either a recent, short-lived burst of star formation that is now in its final phases, or continuous star formation over a relatively short period ($<50\,{\rm Myr}$). GHZ8 therefore provides observational evidence for bursty star formation in a UV-bright galaxy. Globally, GHZ8 may be undergoing a merger-induced starburst, although deeper imaging or spatially resolved kinematic information is required for a more definitive interpretation.



From Dispersion to Metallicity: Probing the Milky Way Circumgalactic Medium with Fast Radio Bursts

Authors: Manami Roy, Sanskriti Das, Smita Mathur, Anjali Gupta, Yair Krongold
Categories: astro-ph.GA
Comments: 8 pages, 3 Figures, and 1 Table (Submitted to ApJ)
Published: 2026-10-29
Matching Keywords: outflow (abstract), fast radio burst (title, abstract), CGM (abstract), circumgalactic medium (title, abstract), IGM (abstract), intergalactic medium (abstract), halo (abstract)

Abstract: Circumgalactic Medium (CGM) plays a critical role in galactic evolution, serving as the primary reservoir for accretion and outflows. In this work, we present a novel statistical analysis to derive CGM metallicity from the dispersion measure (DM) of 25 Fast Radio Burst (FRB) sightlines. By combining metallicity-dependent oxygen absorption DM estimates with metallicity-independent FRB DMs, we directly constrain the metallicity of the hot phase of the Milky Way (MW) CGM. Considering different diffuse baryon fractions ($f_{\text{d}} = 0.0, 0.6, 0.8$) in the Intergalactic Medium (IGM) and intervening halos, the median metallicities of CGM are $Z_{\text{CGM}} = 0.12_{-0.04}^{+0.09} \, Z_{\odot}$, $0.26_{-0.12}^{+0.18} \, Z_{\odot}$, and $0.39_{-0.20}^{+0.26} \, Z_{\odot}$, respectively, by the bootstrapping method. Super-solar metallicity is seen in multiple sightlines for $f_d=0.8$, which is implausible for MW CGM metallicities. Even under conservative lower-bound estimates ($f_{\text{d}} = 0.0$), the inferred CGM metallicity significantly exceeds expectations for IGM accretion ($Z_{\text{IGM}} \lesssim 0.1 \, Z_{\odot}$), providing strong empirical evidence that a large fraction of the metal-enriched galactic feedback material is efficiently retained within the Milky Way CGM. Furthermore, we observe significant sightline-to-sightline variation in inferred metallicities, pointing toward a highly anisotropic multi-phase CGM shaped by complex interactions between feedback outflows and cosmic accretion.

Authors: Karolina Garcia, Anthony H. Gonzalez, Mark Brodwin, Peter Eisenhardt, Tyler George, Nadia Hansen, S. A. Stanford, Daniel Stern, Khunanon Thongkham, Ariane Trudeau, Michael J. I. Brown, Buell T. Jannuzi

Abstract: We present the Infrared Array Camera (IRAC) Distant Cluster Survey (IDCS), a catalog of 825 galaxy cluster and group candidates identified in the Boötes field using the PZWav detection code. Candidates are selected using photometric redshift probability distributions derived from twelve-band optical and infrared imaging. The catalog spans $0.08 < z < 2.0$ (median $z = 0.93$) and includes 147 systems at $z \geq 1.5$. For $z < 1.5$, simulations indicate a purity of $93\%$ at the adopted S/N $\geq 4$ threshold and $50\%$ completeness at $\log(M_{200c}/M_\odot) = 13.74 \pm 0.01$. We validate the cluster photometric redshifts through comparison with spectroscopic data, finding $σ_z/(1+z)=0.017$. Including 18 previously published IRAC Shallow Cluster Survey (ISCS) systems, a total of 80 IDCS cluster candidates are now confirmed through spectroscopy and/or X-ray observations. DESI DR1 spectroscopy provides redshifts for 38 systems, including 23 newly confirmed clusters. These DESI-confirmed systems span $0.12 < z < 0.93$ and have at least 10 spectroscopic members each, from which we measure a median velocity dispersion of $\sim 490$ km s$^{-1}$, corresponding to a median halo mass of $1.1 \times 10^{14} M_\odot$. A positional comparison with 39 independently confirmed literature clusters yields a median centroid offset of 211 kpc. The IDCS catalog provides a cluster sample spanning a wide redshift range, with a selection function characterized through simulations and is a valuable resource for studies of large-scale structure, galaxy evolution in dense environments, and the calibration of cluster detection techniques for current and upcoming wide-field surveys, including LSST, Roman, and Euclid.

Authors: R. Paviot, C. Murray, M. Kilbinger, C. Daley, F. Hervas-Peters, A. Corinaldi, T. de Boer, S. Guerrini, M. J. Hudson, G. S. H. Paek, L. Van Waerbeke, J. Yun

Abstract: Intrinsic alignments (IA) of galaxies are a dominant source of systematics in weak-lensing cosmological analyses, and direct measurements on spectroscopic samples are the primary source of priors on their amplitude. We measure the IA of galaxies from the first data release (DR1) of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) as a function of colour, stellar mass, and halo mass, by cross-correlating the DESI spectroscopic catalogues with the weak-lensing shape catalogue of the Ultraviolet Near-Infrared Optical Northern Survey (UNIONS) over up to 2400 deg$^2$. We fit the three-dimensional galaxy-galaxy and galaxy-shape multipoles of the DESI BGS, LRG, and ELG samples, split by colour and stellar mass, with the non-linear linear alignment (NLA) and the tidal alignment and tidal torquing (TATT) models. Halo masses are measured for the BGS red sample from galaxy-galaxy lensing and extended to the LRGs through the resulting stellar-to-halo mass relation. Red galaxies are strongly and radially aligned, but their alignment amplitude is not a single power law of halo mass between $6\times10^{11}$ and $3\times10^{13}\,h^{-1}M_\odot$: it is nearly flat at low mass and rises steeply above a break at $1.3\times10^{13}\,h^{-1}M_\odot$. Massive blue BGS galaxies instead show a tangential alignment, detected at $3.3σ$, to our knowledge the most significant detection of a non-zero alignment of blue galaxies to date, while the low-mass blue BGS galaxies and the ELGs are consistent with zero. At our current precision, the data do not favour TATT over NLA.

Abstract: Deuteration is a powerful metric to gain insight into the formation pathways of interstellar molecules in star forming regions. Our primary goal is to search for the fully deuterated form of methyl cyanide, CD3CN, in the ISM. We used a sensitive imaging spectral survey of the protostellar system L1551 IRS5 in the Taurus molecular cloud performed as part of the Large Program carried out with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) called Complex Organic Molecules in Protostars with ALMA Spectral Surveys (COMPASS). We derived the column densities of methyl cyanide and several of its less abundant isotopologs, including deuterated ones, under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium. All three deuterated isotopologs of methyl cyanide were detected toward the northern binary component of L1551 IRS5. In particular, we report the first interstellar detection of CD3CN. The measured D/H ratio increases by about half an order of magnitude from one deuterated isotopolog to the next, in a similar way as what was measured for methanol in several other low-mass protostars. This suggests that methyl cyanide predominantly forms like methanol on the surface of dust grains during the cold prestellar phase. However, the measured factor of 2 difference between the D/H ratios of methyl cyanide and methanol may either indicate that gas-phase processes during the warm-up stage contribute approximately equally to the production of methyl cyanide, or that methyl cyanide and its deuterated isotopologs are formed during the prestellar phase at a somewhat earlier time than methanol. Multiply deuterated isotopologs are crucial to understand the formation and inheritance of molecules in the ISM by coupling observations and astrochemical models. Unfortunately, no model has so far investigated the deuteration of methyl cyanide up to the fully deuterated form. [Abridged]



Celia: Catalog Embeddings Learned from cosmologIcal simulAtions

Authors: Xiaowen Zhang, Matthew Ho, Anshul Kumar, Tiziana Di Matteo, Rupert A. C. Croft, Barnabás Póczos
Categories: astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.CO
Comments: 23 pages, 14 figures, 4 tables
Published: 2026-10-29
Matching Keywords: galaxy formation (abstract), halo (abstract)

Abstract: Cosmological hydrodynamical simulations connect observed galaxies to cosmology and the physics of galaxy formation. Each simulation is expensive, and different codes model galaxy formation in different ways. We present Celia (Catalog Embeddings Learned from cosmologIcal simulAtions), a self-supervised foundation model that maps catalogs of subhalos and their galaxy properties to an embedding vector for the purposes of compression and organization of astrophysical and large-scale structure information. Celia is pretrained without parameter labels on the IllustrisTNG, SIMBA and Astrid suites of CAMELS and their N-body counterparts, by reconstructing masked subhalo properties and predicting the representations of a teacher network. Simple linear models fitted to the embeddings recover $Ω_{\mathrm m}$ with $R^2=0.999$, and $σ_8$ and both supernova-feedback amplitudes with $R^2\geq0.97$, in all three suites. The embeddings translate well to out-of-distribution simulations such as Swift-EAGLE, a code that Celia never saw. A linear model fitted on the three training codes predicts its $Ω_{\mathrm m}$ with a correlation coefficient $r=0.985$, and linear models fitted on 20 labeled simulations reach $r\geq0.94$ for $Ω_{\mathrm m}$, $σ_8$ and a supernova-feedback amplitude. We decompose the variation among the simulations of each suite into principal components of the embedding, and simple power laws of the simulation parameters describe the leading components. Celia gives simulated galaxy catalogs a shared coordinate system for simulation-based inference, emulation, the comparison of galaxy-formation models, the detection of physical degeneracies and the discovery of new astrophysical probes.



Can the $\sim 5$ kpc starburst ring in MW create eROSITA bubbles?

Authors: Mukesh Singh Bisht, Kartick C. Sarkar
Categories: astro-ph.GA
Comments: 7 pages, 3 figures, Submitted to ApJ
Published: 2026-10-29
Matching Keywords: outflow (abstract), star formation (abstract)

Abstract: eROSITA bubbles (eRBs) in our Galaxy are thought to be due to the energy injection by the central star-forming region or the central Super Massive Black Hole. Identifying the origin of these bubbles is crucial in constraining the origin of the Fermi Bubbles and the nature of the Galactic feedback. However, some recent opinions suggest that the eRBs may not even have been produced by any central source, but rather a result of a star-formation ring at $R\sim 5$ kpc. In this letter, we perform detailed hydrodynamic simulations to refute that claim and show that such a ring of star formation alone is not able to produce the symmetric eROSITA bubbles - one needs a compact energy injection from the Galactic Center to produce these bubbles. Our study, therefore, suggests that the eROSITA bubbles truly represent the nuclear outflow from our Galaxy.

Abstract: Context. Hickson Compact Groups (HCGs) present an ideal environment for studying galaxy interactions. HCG 16 is a group of five galaxies in an intermediate stage of evolution (Phase 2), with several members displaying signs of interaction, including distorted morphologies, tidal features, and enhanced star formation rates. Aims. We aim to better understand the evolution of HCG 16 and its member galaxies, focusing on the relationship between neutral hydrogen and star formation, while reconstructing the interaction history of the group. Methods. We analysed MUSE data processed with pyPipe3D to extract H$α$ images and stellar population synthesis results, and reduced MeerKAT data to produce a high-resolution HI cube. We examined the structure and dynamics of the H$α$ and HI lines in both 2D and 3D, and produced spatially resolved star formation histories to compare the inner and outer parts of the galaxies. Results. The passage of NGC 848 probably activated star formation in HCG 16, but cannot by itself explain the current starburst activity in HCG 16c and 16d. The evolution of the group is better explained by multiple interactions among its members than by a single strong encounter.

Abstract: We selected 24 nearby late-type galaxies from the MUSE archive with kinematic coverage extending to at least one effective radius. Stellar kinematics were extracted with the GIST pipeline using pPXF, and axisymmetric Schwarzschild models were constructed with \textsc{dynamite}. The stellar orbits of each galaxy were classified by circularity into dynamically cold, warm, hot, and counter-rotating components, and we calculated the corresponding luminosity fractions of each component within $R_{\mathrm{e}}$. Our results confirm the mass-dependent trends in orbital structure previously identified from CALIFA galaxies and reproduced by IllustrisTNG. We regard this as a tentative result, given the limited sample size.



Dwarf Galaxy Reticulum II's MeerKAT L-Band Observation: Constraining WIMP Dark Matter

Authors: Phillip Badenhorst, Michael Sarkis, Yin-Zhe Ma, Wen-Qing Guo, Geoff Beck, Qiang Yuan, Gyula I. G. Jozsa, Steven G. Murray
Categories: astro-ph.CO, hep-ph
Comments: 16 pages, 8 figures
Published: 2026-10-29
Matching Keywords: halo (abstract), dark matter halo (abstract)

Abstract: The nature of dark matter remains one of the most profound open questions in modern physics, with weakly interacting massive particles (WIMPs) among the leading candidates proposed to account for the observed anomalies across cosmic scales. If WIMPs annihilate or decay in the dense dark matter haloes of nearby galaxies, the resulting high-energy electrons/positrons can produce synchrotron radiation in the presence of ambient magnetic fields, yielding a potentially detectable radio signature. Dwarf spheroidal galaxies are particularly promising targets for indirect dark matter searches due to their high mass-to-light ratios and low astrophysical foreground contamination. While radio observations have been widely used to constrain WIMP properties in these systems, the resulting limits remain highly sensitive to poorly constrained magnetic field assumptions. Here we utilize L-band ($1.3$--$1.5$ GHz) observations from the MeerKAT array directed towards dwarf galaxy Reticulum II and measure the surface brightness profile across the dwarf galaxy. Combining these measurements with theoretical models of WIMP annihilation and yield 95% confidence level upper limits on the WIMP annihilation cross-section $\langleσv\rangle \lesssim 10^{-26}$ cm$^3$ s$^{-1}$ for masses $m_χ\sim 10$-$200$ GeV (with the assumed uniform magnetic field model and diffusion constant). As next-generation radio facilities such as the Square Kilometre Array come online, robust characterisation of magnetic environments in dwarf galaxies will be essential to fully exploit their sensitivity to the particle nature of dark matter.



Feeding or Fleeing: The Impact of Enhanced Early Black Hole Growth on Circumgalactic Clumps at $z > 6$

Authors: Floor van Donkelaar, Lucas Tortora, Debora Sijacki
Categories: astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.HE
Comments: Comments and suggestions welcome prior to submission to MNRAS
Published: 2026-10-29
Matching Keywords: outflow (abstract), CGM (abstract), circumgalactic medium (abstract), halo (abstract)

Abstract: The circumgalactic medium (CGM) of massive high-redshift galaxies contains inflowing filamentary gas, recycled material, and feedback-driven outflows. We investigate the formation and evolution of dense CGM clumps in two cosmological zoom-in simulations of the same massive halo at $z > 6$: the fiducial Fable model and the modified Fable-SE model, which includes enhanced early black hole growth through mild super-Eddington accretion. We identify dense gas clumps in the CGM and follow their gas properties, stellar content, and radial evolution across cosmic time. At the resolution probed here, the two simulations show broadly similar distributions of clump gas mass, gas size and associated stellar mass. Instead, the clump populations differ mainly in their abundance, metallicity, and radial evolution. Fable-SE produces substantially more metal-enriched clumps, including an outward-moving population during the strong AGN-driven blow-out phase at $z \lesssim 7$. These systems are associated with disturbed HI-rich gas and relatively high gas densities, linking the increased prevalence of dense, metal-enriched CGM structures in Fable-SE to its stronger AGN-driven blow-out phase. Although inward migration dominates overall, Fable-SE also contains an outward-moving clump population during the blow-out phase, with some clumps moving beyond the virial radius. Our results therefore indicate that the distribution of dense CGM clumps around massive high-redshift galaxies reflects both filamentary accretion and feedback-driven CGM evolution, with strong early AGN activity associated with the outward migration of dense, metal-enriched gas structures containing relatively cool gas.

Abstract: Hoag-type galaxies contain a smooth central core surrounded by a detached outer ring, but their rarity has limited previous studies mainly to individual systems. We identify thirty galaxies with apparent Hoag-type morphology in DESI imaging by visually inspecting two machine-learning ring-galaxy catalogues and analyse their core and ring properties using DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys $g$, $r$, and $z$ images. We measure the rings after subtracting two-dimensional core models and compare the cores with matched SDSS galaxies. The cores lie predominantly on the red sequence, whereas the rings span a broader colour range extending toward the blue cloud. The cores broadly follow early-type mass scaling relations, but their mass-matched distributions tend towards slightly bluer colours, smaller sizes, higher stellar-mass surface densities, and lower concentrations and Sérsic indices. Of the 16 systems with SDSS group information, 14 (87.5 per cent) are centrals, compared with $\sim64\%$ in the mass-matched early-type control sample. The halo-mass and large-scale overdensity distributions become much more similar after central--satellite status is also matched. Ring stellar-mass surface density and normalized width decrease with increasing core-plus-inner-halo to ring mass ratio. Most core $R_{90}$ values lie inside the outermost stable circular orbit of a simplified core-plus-ring model, while the rings frequently extend across its unstable interval. The slightly bluer core colours, lower concentrations, and lower Sérsic indices suggest a possible secular contribution, but the compact, dense cores imply more complex structural assembly than a simple diffuse-pseudobulge origin. The high central fraction may favour gas retention and ring survival by reducing satellite-specific environmental processing. The diversity of ring properties nevertheless leaves room for multiple formation pathways.



Probing Multi-formation Channels of Dark Matter-Deficient Galaxies in a Simulated Universe

Authors: Sanghyeon Han, Ho Seong Hwang, Jaehyun Lee
Categories: astro-ph.GA
Comments: 28 pages, 21 figures, accepted for publication in ApJ
Published: 2026-10-29
Matching Keywords: kinematics (abstract), halo (abstract), dark matter halo (abstract)

Abstract: We investigate the formation and evolution of dark matter-deficient galaxies (DMDGs) using the IllustrisTNG100 cosmological hydrodynamical simulation. We define DMDGs as systems with a dark matter-to-stellar mass ratio below unity, and find two formation channels. In the first, galaxies that initially form within massive dark matter halos lose the majority of their dark matter and transition into DMDGs (acquired DMDGs). In the second, galaxies form with little or no dark matter from the beginning (inborn DMDGs). The two populations show distinct physical properties. Acquired DMDGs exhibit significant temporal variations in mass content, size, and kinematics, whereas inborn DMDGs show mild evolution. The former population tends to form closer to their nearest neighbor galaxies in large-scale overdense regions than the latter population does. We find that most stars in the inborn DMDGs form in situ. Their precursor gas is already metal-enriched and associated with other galaxies before formation. Tidal interactions and filamentary gas flows may contribute to the assembly of compact gas clumps, suggesting multiple formation pathways for inborn DMDGs. Our findings support the idea that there are at least two distinct channels to make galaxies dark matter deficient, which is important for understanding the formation mechanisms of these extreme systems.

Abstract: Leo T, a Milky Way (MW) satellite, is the lowest-mass gas-rich galaxy known to show recent star formation, making it an excellent laboratory for investigating star formation in the ultraviolet (UV) regime within a low-density, metal-poor environment. We present a far-UV (FUV) study using Ultraviolet Imaging Telescope (UVIT) data, aiming to characterize FUV star-forming clusters (SFCs), identify FUV-emitting stars, constrain their properties, and explore star formation in an extremely low-mass, metal-poor galaxy. FUV-emitting stars and their stellar parameters were identified through cross-matching with catalogs from the Hubble Space Telescope and the Multi Unit Spectroscopic Explorer (MUSE). The FUV luminosities of the SFCs confirm the absence of massive O and B-type stars in Leo T. The FUV-emitting stellar population consists of main-sequence (MS) stars, blue loop (BL) stars, horizontal branch stars, and emission-line (EM) stars. We find that five out of the six FUV-emitting stars with MUSE spectroscopy are extremely metal-poor ([Fe/H] $\leq$ -3). The (NUV - r) color profile indicates that the UV emission is dominated by stellar populations younger than 1 Gyr out to a radius $\lt$ 185 pc. Despite the recent detection of CO(1-0) emission, no SFCs are associated with the molecular clouds, which exhibit extremely low SFEs. The spatial distribution of the SFCs suggests a directional interaction with the MW.

Abstract: While numerous studies have shown that satellite galaxies experience declining star formation rates after infall, direct comparisons across different surveys and redshifts are complicated by varying sample selections, environmental metrics, and techniques used to derive galaxy properties. In this work, we select satellite galaxies from the highly complete Galaxy and Mass Assembly (GAMA) Survey and the Deep Extragalactic VIsible Legacy Survey (DEVILS) to investigate environmental quenching out to z~0.4. UnEZ: A Fully Unsupervised Self-Templating Neural Network for Redshift and Line Strength Estimation

Authors: Asha Abd, Karl Glazebrook, Colin Jacobs
Categories: astro-ph.IM, astro-ph.GA, cs.LG
Comments: 22 pages, 17 figures. Submitted to MNRAS
Published: 2026-10-28
Matching Keywords: SDSS (abstract), star formation rate (abstract), star formation (abstract), absorption line (abstract)

Abstract: As spectroscopic surveys grow, estimating galaxy properties such as metallicity, star formation rate, and redshift becomes computationally cumbersome. Methods of estimating such properties rely on template spectra or curated redshift catalogs, which fail to represent the diversity of objects seen in large-scale surveys. We present UnEZ, a fully unsupervised, physics-informed machine learning model that can predict spectroscopic redshifts and line strengths with no prior redshift or line strength labels. An encoder predicts emission and absorption line amplitudes/widths, and a fixed deterministic decoder constructs a unique template, estimated redshift, redshift confidence curve, and spectral model. UnEZ was tested on the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) Data Release 18 due to its large dataset and thoroughly vetted redshift catalog which serves as an independent benchmark. Despite the lack of templates, line strength labels, or redshift labels, UnEZ's performance competes with modern redshift estimation algorithms. At SNR > 10, UnEZ achieves an outlier fraction of 0.459% and a normalized median absolute deviation (NMAD) of $1.237 \times 10^{-4}$. At SNR > 3, UnEZ achieves an outlier fraction of 3.89% and an NMAD of $1.471 \times 10^{-4}$. Furthermore, we demonstrate how UnEZ's line strength and redshift predictions can be utilized for various purposes such as constructing template spectra required in traditional redshift estimation algorithms. UnEZ illustrates how a fully unsupervised neural network can allow redshift, among other galaxy properties, to emerge naturally from the spectra themselves, providing a template-free, label-free approach that scales to the demands of future surveys.
