A Robust Estimator for the Lyman-$α$ Forest 1D Power Spectrum at High Redshift

Authors: Molly Wolfson, Joseph F. Hennawi, Frederick B. Davies, Zarija Lukić
Categories: astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA
Comments: 30 pages, 13 figures
Published: 2026-10-22
Matching Keywords: quasar spectra (abstract), high redshift (title, abstract), quasar (abstract), intergalactic medium (abstract)

Abstract: The Lyman-$α$ (Ly$α$) forest flux power spectrum $P(k)$ is sensitive to a range of astrophysical and cosmological effects, including the thermal state of and large-scale fluctuations in the intergalactic medium and the nature of dark matter. Measuring $P(k)$ becomes increasingly challenging at high redshift, where fewer quasar spectra are available and higher Ly$α$ optical depths result in lower transmitted flux. In addition, observational effects and missing data complicate standard Fourier-transform estimators. Here, we validate a likelihood-based estimator for $P(k)$. The method constructs the pixel-level covariance matrix of the transmitted flux field using precomputed $k$-space integrals that account for instrumental resolution and pixelization, enabling direct maximum-likelihood inference of $P(k)$. We test the estimator using mock Ly$α$ forest spectra at $z \geq 5$, including analytic Gaussian realizations generated from known power spectra and forward-modeled simulation spectra incorporating observational effects relevant to high-redshift quasar surveys, such as finite spectral resolution, pixel sampling, noise, and missing data. We compare the recovered power spectra with both the input spectra and standard Fourier-transform estimates. We find that the likelihood-based estimator recovers the input power spectrum across the tested scales well within expected error and remains robust to the observational effects considered. This work establishes a framework for measuring Ly$α$ forest clustering with a robust, nearly optimal power spectrum estimator, with particular focus on quantifying large-scale fluctuations that may arise from ultraviolet background fluctuations or the late stages of reionization.



SpecLess: A Multi Survey Python Package for Machine Learning Classification of Galaxy Morphology and Activity from Photometry

Authors: Uzay Aydin, Mehmet Tanriver
Categories: astro-ph.GA
Comments: 14 pages, 2 figures, 15 tables. Submitted to Astronomy & Computing. SpecLess v0.3.0. Open source Python package available atthis https URL
Published: 2026-10-22
Matching Keywords: DESI (abstract), SDSS (abstract), morphology (title, abstract)

Abstract: Broad band photometry enables scalable galaxy classification without spectroscopy at inference time, yet strong within survey performance may not transfer when the same galaxies are measured by another survey. We present SpecLess, an open source Python package implementing two photometric classification tasks: high confidence Galaxy Zoo 1 elliptical and spiral morphologies, and four SDSS spectroscopic activity reference classes (starforming, starburst, Seyfert, and LINER). Using SDSS+AllWISE, KiDS+VIKING, and DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys photometry, survey specific XGBoost classifiers achieve held out macro F1 scores of 0.9332 (SDSS; 35,554 galaxies) and 0.9237 (KiDS; 3,269) for morphology, and 0.7637 (SDSS; 16,931) and 0.7398 (DESI; 11,975) for activity. Starforming and starburst references are recovered more effectively than Seyfert and LINER, while feature ablations reveal complementary contributions from optical and mid infrared photometry. We quantify catalogue dependence using separately trained common feature models evaluated on matched galaxies with identical labels and data partitions. Relative to target trained models, macro F1 deficits are 0.0153 (SDSS to KiDS) and 0.0354 (KiDS to SDSS) for morphology (12,301 galaxies; 20 splits), and 0.0660 (SDSS to DESI) and 0.0406 (DESI to SDSS) for activity (11,975 test galaxies). Colour only refits, additive corrections, and optical infrared substitutions reveal direction dependent sensitivity without consistently recovering target trained performance. SpecLess v0.3.0 distributes four pretrained, survey specific classifiers through a unified Python interface. The package provides a reproducible baseline for photometric galaxy classification across multiple surveys while establishing an extensible, open source foundation for future developments in classification methods and photometric catalogue support.



A Catalog of Spectral Properties for DESI DR1 Quasars

Authors: Qiaoya Wu, Yue Shen
Categories: astro-ph.GA
Comments: 17 pages, 10 figures, and 2 tables. Preprint version; comments and feedback are welcome
Published: 2026-10-22
Matching Keywords: DESI (title, abstract), quasar (title, abstract)

Abstract: The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) is carrying out a five-year spectroscopic survey designed to map the large-scale structure of the Universe and obtain spectra for millions of galaxies and quasars over a wide redshift range. We present a value-added catalog of spectral properties for 1,717,774 unique quasars (1,774,723 spectra) in the DESI Data Release 1. We perform a uniform spectral decomposition of the quasar continuum, host galaxy emission (at $z<1$), and \FeII\ emission, and measure the line properties of major broad and narrow emission lines from Ly$α$ to H$α$ covered by DESI spectra. We also provide single-epoch black hole mass estimates (from H$β$, \MgII, and \CIV), bolometric luminosities, and Eddington ratios for these quasars. We provide systemic redshifts that account for the intrinsic velocity shifts of individual lines; they agree with the DESI pipeline redshifts to within 500~km s$^{-1}$ for 88.6\% of the sample, and are most useful at $z>1$ where the pipeline redshift is heavily constrained by blueshifted high-ionization lines. We also provide local fits to several optical coronal lines, including \NeV~$\lambda3426$, [\ion{Fe}{7}]~$\lambda5722$, [\ion{Fe}{7}]~$\lambda6088$, and [\ion{Fe}{11}]~$\lambda7893$. We show that several emission-line ratios, including those involving coronal lines, vary systematically with Eddington ratio. The catalog is intended as a community resource for studies of quasar demographics, black hole accretion, emission-line physics, and the connection between the broad-, coronal-, and narrow-line regions.



A cosmic filament catalog for COSMOS-Web Data Release 1 across 0.5 < z < 3.5

Authors: Qiaoyang Hao, Tao Wang, Weishan Zhu, Hong Guo, Peng Wang, Tiancheng Yang, Luwenjia Zhou

Comments: 21 pages, 18 figures, to be published in A&A
Published: 2026-10-22
Matching Keywords: galaxy evolution (abstract), high redshift (abstract)

Abstract: Cosmic filaments represent the environments in which most galaxies form and evolve. However, the spatial distribution of filaments and the link between galaxy properties and their positions relative to filaments remains unclear, particularly at high redshifts. In this work, using the deep-field catalog COSMOS-Web Data Release 1, we construct a filament catalog spanning 0.5 < z < 3.5 that can be directly applied to studies of galaxy evolution and the galaxy--large-scale-structure connection at the peak epoch of galaxy and black hole formation. We developed a new procedure based on the DisPerSE method that fully incorporates the probability distribution functions (PDFs) of galaxy photometric redshifts by generating multiple realizations for each tomographic 2D slice. We then stacked these realizations to construct ``filament probability maps'' (w_ fil maps), from which filaments were subsequently extracted. The filament extraction parameters were optimized using mock catalogs generated from TNG simulations by minimizing the discrepancies between 3D and projected 2D filaments. Applying this procedure to the COSMOS-Web data, we detected 12,523 filaments (comprising 136,210 segments) across 0.5 < z < 3.5 and released a filament catalog, a companion segment catalog, and filament probability maps for each slice. Validation against astrophysical properties shows a strong agreement between the extracted filaments and those in the simulations, supporting the robustness of the filament extraction. These data products provide a robust foundation for investigating galaxy--environment coevolution at cosmic noon and beyond.

The LBT Y$_{\rm p}$ Project VI: Gas-Phase Chemical Abundance Patterns of Metal-Poor Nebulae

Authors: Noah S. J. Rogers, Evan D. Skillman, Richard W. Pogge, Erik Aver, Miqaela K. Weller, Danielle A. Berg, John J. Salzer, John H. Miller, Jayde Speigel, Allison L. Strom

Abstract: Through a combined rest-optical and NIR observing campaign, the Large Binocular Telescope Primordial He Abundance ($Y_{\rm p}$) Project has delivered a precision measurement of $Y_{\rm p}$. Given the stringent requirements to measure gas-phase He abundances, the optical spectra contain emission features from a plethora of metals in the Interstellar Medium (ISM). We present gas-phase N, O, Ne, S, Cl, Ar, K and Fe abundance patterns in 62 low-metallicity nebulae. We assess ionization correction factors for each element and select those that reproduce the observed ionic abundance trends. The Ne/O, S/O, and Ar/O abundances do not evolve with metallicity and agree with the solar ratios. Given the Type Ia enrichment of S and Ar, the solar S/O and Ar/O suggest extended star formation histories in local galaxies relative to high-$z$ galaxies at fixed O/H. We report the first ISM K abundances in low-metallicity extragalactic nebulae; both Cl/O and K/O are constant with O/H, indicating the production of these odd-Z elements follows that of $α$ elements. Consistent with prior analyses, gas-phase Fe/O is observed to decrease with O/H due to dust depletion; correcting for this produces an average [Fe/O]$=$$-$0.80, implying Fe-deficient stars capable of producing hard ionizing spectra. Finally, the metallicity-insensitive log(N/O) plateau from 50 nebulae is $-$1.43$\pm$0.10, consistent with previous works. Five galaxies have N/O significantly enhanced relative to the primary plateau, and we discuss their properties, mechanisms that could bias measurements of N/O, and nucleosynthetic progenitors that can produce enhanced N at low metallicity.

Authors: Parth Mehrotra, Aaron Smith, Yuan Bian, William McClymont, Ewald Puchwein, Rahul Kannan, Laura Keating, Xuejian Shen, Mark Vogelsberger, Sandro Tacchella, Matthew Hayes, Crystal L. Martin, Cody Carr, Anand Utsav Kapoor, Shyam Menon, Alaina Henry
Categories: astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.CO
Comments: 26 pages, 24 figures. Comments welcome
Published: 2026-10-21
Matching Keywords: star formation (abstract), stellar feedback (abstract), halo (abstract)

Abstract: The ionizing radiation that galaxies release into their surroundings depends on whether efficient Lyman continuum (LyC) escape coincides with strong photon production. Stellar feedback can open escape paths as the source population fades, linking the escaping radiation to a galaxy's recent history. We investigate this timing in thirteen high-redshift (z > 3) target haloes from the THESAN-ZOOM radiation-hydrodynamic simulations, post-processed with COLT radiative transfer and sampled at ~10 Myr intervals. The clearest delayed relationship links $\text{SFR}_{10}/\text{SFR}_{100}$ to the LyC escape fraction. Selecting the nearest positive cross-correlation peak gives typical lags of ~16 Myr and peak correlations of 0.4-0.6 in massive haloes, with longer and less coherent delays towards lower masses. The fitted delays scale approximately as $M_\star^{-0.19}$ and $M_\text{halo}^{-0.27}$, although the shortest lags approach the native snapshot spacing. Recurrent variations span multiple amplitude-dependent timescales, consistent with repeated episodes of star formation and feedback. Recent star formation closely tracks both intrinsic and escaping photon rates, while the escape fraction reflects the evolving gas transparency. The H$α$-to-UV luminosity ratio retains a delayed association with escape, offering an observable connection between burst phase and leakage. High transparency during a fading phase can nevertheless yield little escaping radiation. The temporal overlap of photon production and transmission therefore shapes both the amount and timing of escaping radiation, informing the interpretation of observed leakers and their contribution to cosmic reionization.

Abstract: We investigate how cluster environments affect galaxy star formation, structure, and stellar populations using spectroscopically-confirmed galaxies in X-ray-selected eROSITA clusters, that have deep HSC-SSP imaging and DESI DR1 spectroscopy. At 0.1 < z < 0.5, we compare dust-corrected Ha star formation rates (SFRs), effective radii (Re), bulge-to-total light ratios (B/T), and Dn4000 between cluster satellites and a stellar-mass- and redshift-matched field control sample. We identify UVJ-quiescent galaxies and divide the UVJ star-forming population at log(sSFR_Ha)= -10.5 yr-1 into actively star-forming and Ha-low systems, the latter referred to as green-valley (GV) galaxies. Cluster--field differences depend strongly on stellar mass, projected cluster-centric radius, and galaxy population. Cluster star-forming galaxies show modest SFR suppression but weak structural offsets. The strongest coupled signatures occur among Ha-low UVJ-star-forming galaxies, particularly low-mass satellites at R<0.5R500, which have lower SFRs, smaller light-weighted sizes, larger B/T, and larger Dn4000 than matched field counterparts. The multivariate offset is largest for low-mass GV galaxies and decreases toward larger projected radius. The broader radial extent of SFR suppression relative to structural offsets suggests that star-formation suppression has a broader environmental reach than structural transformation. These results show that environmental quenching and structural transformation are most strongly coupled in the Ha-low transition population.

Abstract: JWST is revealing abundant populations of high-$z$ galaxies with unforeseen properties: UV-luminous galaxies with high nitrogen enrichment, populations of very massive stars (VMSs; $>100\ M_\odot$), and early forms of massive black hole growth such as the Little Red Dots (LRDs).
