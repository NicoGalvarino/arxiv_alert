Cosmological Inference with Gravitational-Wave Dark Sirens and 21-cm Intensity Mapping: Forecasts for LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA with MeerKAT and SKAO

Authors: Dounia Nanadoumgar-Lacroze, Konstantin Leyde, Steven Cunnington, Tessa Baker, Ian Harrison, Ulyana Dupletsa, Simone Mastrogiovanni, Riccardo Murgia, Tommaso Ronconi, Matteo Schulz, Marta Spinelli
Categories: astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
Comments: 17 pages, 9 figures. Submitted to MNRAS. Comments are welcome
Published: 2026-10-22
Matching Keywords: multimodal (abstract), multimodality (abstract)

Abstract: Gravitational waves (GWs) and 21-cm intensity mapping (IM) are emerging as complementary cosmological probes: GWs provide direct luminosity-distance measurements, while neutral-hydrogen (HI) IM traces the matter distribution in redshift space. Their synergy offers a promising avenue for precise measurements of the Hubble constant. Within a standard-siren framework, we perform a joint cosmological and population analysis using $\mathcal{O}(150)$ mock GW detections with SNR > 12, as projected for one year of observations at LVK O5 sensitivity. We combine these with mock HI intensity maps from MeerKLASS and SKA-Mid, and study how observational limitations in both the GW and IM data affect the $H_0$ constraints. We find that uncertainty in GW parameter measurements is a dominant limitation on the cosmological constraints. Neglecting this uncertainty, the inclusion of HI information enables sub-percent constraints on $H_0$. We also find that observational effects in the HI surveys weaken the constraints, although they remain tighter than those obtained without HI information (spectral sirens). Despite accounting for HI IM observational limitations, including HI information reduces the $H_0$ uncertainty relative to the spectral-siren results by 61% for MeerKLASS and 53% for SKA-Mid. Because our results are based on a single realisation, they should be interpreted in light of possible realisation-to-realisation variability. Beam convolution most severely weakens the constraints. Instrumental noise can induce multimodality in the posterior, potentially biasing the inferred parameters. The results are sensitive to foreground under-cleaning and over-cleaning. Partial sky coverage has a relatively small impact. In the most realistic scenario, which accounts for both GW and HI IM limitations, we obtain $H_0$ precision estimates of 10% and 15% for MeerKLASS and SKA-Mid, respectively.



Data-driven characterisation of variable source light curves with multimodal representation learning

Authors: M. Madarász, G. Marton, M. Audard
Categories: astro-ph.IM, astro-ph.SR
Comments: 20 pages, 13 figures, 12 tables, accepted for publication in A&A
Published: 2026-10-21
Matching Keywords: data-driven (title), convolutional (abstract), neural network (abstract), multimodal (title, abstract), transformer (abstract)

Abstract: Surveys such as the Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) and the Vera C. Rubin Observatory Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) produce light curves for hundreds of millions of variables. Their characterisation traditionally relies on supervised classifiers with hand-crafted features and curated labels, limiting them to predefined taxonomies and individual surveys. We present a general, survey-agnostic framework that characterises variable sources without labelled training data, producing low-dimensional representations of light curve properties such as amplitude, periodicity, and asymmetry. A multimodal architecture combines two complementary views, a Transformer encoder on raw observations and a convolutional neural network on a generalised Lomb-Scargle periodogram. Each light curve is randomly split into two temporal halves as independent views, and the branches are trained jointly with VICReg to produce embeddings invariant across halves while keeping each dimension informative. We evaluate on 37 006 ZTF sources spanning 14 variable star classes in four broad physical categories. A linear classifier on the frozen 64-dimensional embeddings achieves 77.1% balanced accuracy for classes and 90.5% for broad categories. Combining both branches improves balanced accuracy by up to 14.9 percentage points over the best single modality, confirming their complementarity. Interpretable light curve properties are encoded on distinct dimensions, and within-class analysis reveals physical substructure unresolved by reference labels. Retraining on ZTF g and i bands without modification reproduces the main results, and concatenating multi-band embeddings improves balanced accuracy to 86.1%. The framework needs no labels, phase folding, or survey-specific preprocessing and applies directly to other surveys with different cadences and wavelength coverage.



A Radiation-Hardened, Fault-Aware Quantized Neural Network That Self-Heals

Authors: Peter Xiangyuan Ma, Joshua S. Bloom
Categories: astro-ph.IM, cs.LG, hep-ex, nucl-ex, physics.ins-det
Comments: 22 pages, 4 figures, 11 tables
Published: 2026-10-20
Matching Keywords: data-driven (abstract), neural network (title, abstract)

Abstract: Deep learning (DL) models are increasingly being deployed into radiation-challenging environments in spacecraft, particle accelerators, and, soon, space-based data centers. Instantiated as a learned orchestration of numbers stored in persistent memory, models are susceptible to continual degradation as high-energy particles corrupt data. At inference time, new data passed through the network fundamentally break the off-line assumptions and guarantees of a static model determined during training. To preserve the quality and efficacy of the models, fault tolerance against bit flips is a clear objective. In this work we developed a runtime method for repairing the quantized weights of neural networks (NNs) after bit flips on commodity, commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) hardware, without storing extra replicas. Our work extends the well-established Fault-Aware Training (FAT) paradigm by adding an alignment penalty to concentrate undamaged activations and weights into a low-rank subspace, and we then reconstruct flagged corrupted coordinates from the uncorrupted survivors. We call this method {\it Projection Healing} and apply it to six regression benchmarks on quantized NNs. Holding a FAT checkpoint fixed and toggling only the runtime heal gives a further ${\sim}23\%$ reduction in the native-unit excess integral. Across those datasets the method extends the operational scrub interval by a median of $2.04\times$ at a $+5\%$ RMSE degradation and $1.96\times$ at $+50\%$, compared with $1.62\times$ and $1.07\times$ for classical Single Error Correction, Double Error Detection (SECDED). This introduces a new path towards designing resilient data-driven models, complementing established hardware redundancy, ECC, and FAT-based techniques.



Graph Neural Networks for Astrophysics and Cosmology (seen before)

Authors: Christian Kragh Jespersen
Categories: astro-ph.IM, astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA, cs.LG
Comments: Main text contains 43 pages, 17 figures, and 2 tables. Invited review chapter for the edited book Machine Learning Techniques for Astrophysics and Cosmology (Eds. Cosimo Bambi, Vinay Kashyap, Swarnim Shashank, Naoki Yoshida, Springer)
Published: 2026-10-19
Matching Keywords: convolutional (abstract), neural network (title, abstract), transformer (abstract)

Abstract: Physics seldom makes breakthroughs when we consider objects only in isolation. Instead we model and measure how objects sit among their neighbours, spatial, temporal, or otherwise: a galaxy in its environment, a halo in its history, a photon hit in a detector. Graphs are ideally suited for encoding exactly these relations, and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) learn the geometric functions that respect them. This review presents the use of GNNs for astrophysics and cosmology, and argues that GNNs often suit physics and physical data better than any alternative: they fit the native geometry of the domain and the signal, enable the incorporation of the symmetries and prior knowledge we already trust and have toiled to build, and stay flexible enough to capture what we do not yet understand. GNNs' physically intuitive workings also render them more interpretable than other deep-learning architectures. We show how we can begin from general principles of the geometry of deep learning and build the graph and the graph neural network from the ground up, and how other architectures, like the convolutional network and the transformer, can be seen as special cases of one unified geometric blueprint. We survey applications across cosmology, galaxy formation, dynamics, and instrumentation; and we chart the frontier. From this survey, we find that only one in six articles outside instrumentation applies GNNs to data rather than just simulations. We also distil a set of best practices, including when not to reach for a graph. Throughout, we will prioritise physical intuition and foundational principles over mathematical machinery whenever possible.



Plate-YOLO: An End-to-End Object Detection Framework for Robust Astrometric Registration of Historical Plates (seen before)

Authors: Quanfeng Xu, Zhengjun Shang, Shiyin Shen, Yong Yu, Mingyang Ma, Zhenghong Tang, Meiting Yang, Jing Yang, Jianhai Zhao
Categories: astro-ph.IM
Comments: 14 pages, 6 figures, accepted for publication in The Astronomical Journal
Published: 2026-10-19
Matching Keywords: time-domain (abstract), attention (abstract)

Abstract: Historical astronomical plates provide a long temporal baseline for time-domain astrophysics, but degradation and structural artifacts can hinder automated astrometric calibration. We present Plate-YOLO, a framework that combines source detection and stellar classification in one detector and supplies source catalogs to a separate astrometric solver. The detector adapts YOLOv5 with Space-to-Depth feature rearrangement, SimAM attention, a high-resolution P2 head, and a Normalized Wasserstein Distance loss for small bounding boxes. A Gaia-guided fine-tuning procedure uses initially solved plates to construct labels for a new archive. On 150 UBAI plates for which the conventional pipeline had failed, the Chinese-trained model solved 40 plates, and target-archive fine-tuning recovered a further 106, giving an overall recovery rate of 146/150 (97.3%). In the reported hardware configuration, source extraction took 6.61 s per plate for Plate-YOLO and 16.73 s for SExtractor. These results demonstrate the practical value of adapting source detection to degraded historical plates.
