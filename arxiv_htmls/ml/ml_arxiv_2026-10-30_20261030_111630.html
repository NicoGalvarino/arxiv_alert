Field-level inference under pressure: the information content of the thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich field

Authors: Carmen Embil Villagra, Adrian E. Bayer, William R. Coulton, Alina Sabyr, Francisco Villaescusa-Navarro, Blake D. Sherwin, Anthony Challinor, J. Colin Hill, Nicholas Battaglia
Categories: astro-ph.CO
Comments: 16 pages, 6 figures
Published: 2026-10-29
Matching Keywords: convolutional (abstract), neural network (abstract)

Abstract: The thermal Sunyaev--Zel'dovich (tSZ) field is highly non-Gaussian and contains cosmological information that cannot be fully captured by its angular power spectrum. In this work, we investigate the information content of this field using a convolutional neural network (CNN), and compare to simple and interpretable summary statistics, in a simulation-based inference (SBI) framework. We perform the first field-level analysis of the tSZ field, using a suite of $10{,}000$ simulated Compton-$y$ maps, where we jointly vary the cosmological parameters $Ω_c$ and $σ_8$ (the density fraction for cold dark matter and the matter clustering amplitude, respectively) and two parameters describing the amplitude and shape of the electron pressure profile, $P_0$ and $β$, that aim to capture astrophysical uncertainties. We consider both noiseless simulations and simulations including realistic Simons Observatory (SO) noise after component separation. We use SBI to compare the constraints obtained from the CNN with those from the three Minkowski functionals (MFs), individually and in combination, as well as the angular power spectrum. This is the first application of SBI to the MFs of the tSZ field. We find that the CNN provides tighter constraints than conventional summary statistics in the noiseless case. However, at SO-like noise levels, the combination of MFs and the power spectrum recovers constraints comparable to those obtained with the CNN. Our results highlight the potential of these interpretable summary statistics, as well as field-level inference, for extracting information from the tSZ field, motivating efforts toward their application to real data.



OCCAMS: A Multi-Task Learning-Based Investigation of the CMB Power Spectra

Authors: Ankit Mandal, Rahul Shah, Md. Wali Hossain, Somasri Sen, Anjan A Sen, Supratik Pal
Categories: astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.IM, cs.LG
Comments: 13 pages, 4 figures, 4 tables, plus appendices. Comments are welcome
Published: 2026-10-29
Matching Keywords: data-driven (abstract), variational autoencoder (abstract), neural network (abstract)

Abstract: How many latent variables are needed to fit the cosmic microwave background (CMB) temperature and polarisation power spectra when a neural network is given no cosmological model, and to what extent do they map onto the standard cosmological parameters? We use multi-task learning with variational autoencoders trained on CLASS-generated spectra (TT, TE, EE) over $2\le\ell\le2500$. No cosmological model is built into the network, and the number of latent variables, $L$, is varied over $L=5$, $6$ and $7$. Their fits are compared to the Planck 2018 data using $\hatχ^2$, AIC, BIC and Bayesian evidence. Their correspondence with cosmological parameters is measured with normalised mutual information post-training. The loss weights are set within a single training run by uncertainty weighting, with the weight of the disentanglement term either fixed (semi-automated, SA) or learned (completely automated, CA). The reconstruction loss is split over multipole ranges in three configurations that separate the roles of temperature and polarisation and of the low- and high-$\ell$ ranges. In four of the six combinations of configuration and strategy, the fit improves from $L=5$ to $L=6$ and not beyond, and $L=6$ is the best, or close to the best, in five of the six cases. So, six latent variables are needed, as many as the free parameters of the standard, model-dependent approach. SA gives clearer disentanglement, while CA needs no a priori choice of the disentanglement weight and has the higher evidence at $L=5$ and $6$: the two strategies are points on a trade-off between interpretability and fit to the data. Notably, the latent variables for $τ$ and $A_s$ share almost no information in any model, consistent with large-scale polarisation separating the two, as in standard analyses. A deep-learning approach can thus give a compact, data-driven parametrisation of the CMB spectra.



Parameter-Decoupled Superresolution with Airy-Disk-Informed Learning: Application to NGC 4486B

Authors: James Qu, Isabella L. Lee, Gang Zhang, Abdelali Sajia, Yashashree Jadhav, Xiao-Feng Qian
Categories: physics.optics, astro-ph.GA
Comments: 9 pages, 4 figures
Published: 2026-10-26
Matching Keywords: convolutional (abstract), neural network (abstract)

Abstract: Estimating the separation of two unresolved or nearly unresolved sources from recorded intensity images is difficult when source brightness, mutual coherence, and phase are unknown and the point-spread function (PSF) departs from a Gaussian. We formulate Hermite--Gaussian (HG) projections of one- and two-dimensional Airy amplitudes and prove that ratios of mode probabilities with equal longitudinal parity exactly cancel the brightness, coherence, and phase nuisance parameters under a symmetric two-source model. For a one-dimensional Airy slice we achieve an explicit multi-ratio separation inversion. For the physical two-dimensional circular-pupil PSF we obtain exact finite-pupil overlap integrals and locally invertible separation responses. Motivated by these analytical invariants, we examine Airy-informed convolutional neural networks (CNNs) on synthetic Airy images with known separations. At 150,000 training images, the approximate mean absolute errors are 0.018 in plotted separation units, 10 times better comparing to 0.18 for non-Airy Gaussian PSF approximation. Applied to an archival Hubble Space Telescope image of the double nucleus of NGC 4486B, the Airy-informed CNN gives estimates of approximately 9.6--12.0 pc for training sets of 100,000--150,000 images, broadly consistent with the historical approximately 12 pc value. We distinguish exact optical-mode nuisance cancellation from empirical robustness of an image-only learned estimator and identify the limits imposed by PSF mismatch and extended galaxy structure.



Parameter-Decoupling-Informed Learning for Superresolution: Laboratory Validation and Galaxy Imaging (seen before)

Authors: Abdelali Sajia, Bilal Benzimoun, James Qu, Pawan Khatiwada, Guogan Zhao, Gang Zhang, Xiao-Feng Qian
Categories: physics.optics, astro-ph.GA
Comments: 10 pages, 5 figures
Published: 2026-10-26
Matching Keywords: data-driven (abstract), convolutional (abstract), neural network (abstract)

Abstract: We present a parameter-decoupling-informed learning model for estimating subwavelength separations of point sources, without requiring prior knowledge of source properties, control over the sources, or continued access to their light signals. The method builds on our parameter-decoupled quantum superresolution estimation theory, which circumvents the need to estimate nuisance parameters such as partial coherence, brightness imbalance, and relative phase. Our convolutional neural network further accommodates practical imperfections, including background noise, photon loss, and centroid and orientation misalignment. We validate the method using synthetic and experimentally generated images, achieving separation-estimation fidelity above $82\%$ for separations as small as approximately $0.075σ$, where $σ$ is the Gaussian intensity-PSF standard deviation. This corresponds to an illustrative separation of $\sim13.5$ nm when $σ=180$ nm, suggesting potential relevance to length scales addressed by advanced active-source microscopy. Applied to archival Hubble images of NGC~4486B, the model yields nuclear separations of 9.3-13.2~pc, consistent with the previously reported value of 12~pc. Comparisons using the same training and testing images demonstrate substantially higher fidelity than conventional direct data-driven learning in the tested cases. The method's robustness to varying source properties and imaging imperfections supports retrospective astrophysical image analysis and motivates applications in live-cell microscopy and quantum metrology where source control or renewed optical measurements are impractical.



The biases caused by interpolated table models and their impact on X-ray spectroscopy (seen before)

Authors: Matteo Lucchini, Benjamin Ricketts, Phil Uttley, Daniela Huppenkothen, Fergus Baker, Gregoire Marcel, Guglielmo Mastroserio
Categories: astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.IM
Comments: Submitted to A&A, comments and discussion welcome
Published: 2026-10-26
Matching Keywords: multimodal (abstract), multi-modal (abstract), multi-modality (abstract)

Abstract: Models for X-ray spectroscopy involving complex relativistic or plasma physics are commonly pre-computed on a grid, In this paper, we aim to quantify the potential biases and distortions introduced by the computational error and propagated into parameter inference by interpolating linearly these complex, non-linear models. We analyse the performance of Bayesian sampling algorithms on mock inference problems. Then, we model real observations of an accreting black hole X-ray binary in both the soft and hard states. We find that linear interpolation can a) greatly bias the posterior distribution of model parameters and b) distort the likelihood space to such a degree that posterior distributions become artificially multimodal. This multi-modality causes a noticeable loss in computational efficiency in both sampling and fitting techniques. As a result, exploring a given parameter space until convergence requires an extremely large computational cost. These findings call for improving the computational techniques used when fitting modern X-ray data, for example through better sampling algorithms and/or more sophisticated interpolation schemes



Comparison of Hybrid and Standard Reconstruction Methods on IXPE Astrophysical Data (seen before)

Authors: Riccardo Ferrazzoli, Nicolò Cibrario Stefano Silvestri, Michela Negro, Paolo Soffitta, Sudip Chakraborty, Simone Pagliarella, Steven R. Ehlert
Categories: astro-ph.IM, astro-ph.HE
Comments: Accepted for publication on A&A. 10 pages, 5 figures
Published: 2026-10-26
Matching Keywords: convolutional (abstract), neural network (abstract)

Abstract: The hybrid reconstruction algorithm developed by Cibrario et al. 2023 combined a convolutional neural network (CNN) with standard moment analysis to improve track reconstruction in IXPE Gas Pixel Detectors. Although validated on laboratory data (Cibrario et al. 2025), the method's astrophysical performance had not been systematically characterized. We present the first comparison of hybrid and standard reconstruction on IXPE point sources, measuring sensitivity gains versus energy. We analyze Her X-1, a bright, polarized accreting pulsar as a benchmark, and soft-state XTE J1701-461, a bright neutron-star low-mass X-ray binary with no detected polarization, to assess residual systematics. From both pipelines' Level-2 event files, we compare normalized Stokes parameters, polarization degree (PD) and angle (PA), and detection significance. For Her X-1, hybrid reconstruction increases significance at all energies, significantly above ~2.83 keV. R = σ_hyb/σ_std differs from unity by 9.3σ in the 2.83-4.00 keV bin and reaches 1.18\pm0.03 at 5.66-8.00 keV (6.2σ). For XTE J1701-461, PD is consistent with zero and R with unity. Fourier analysis reveals excess power in hybrid angle distributions at low energies for modes m = 3, 6, 12 and 15, significant at >7σ relative to the standard method; at 5-8 keV, all modes are consistent with noise in both methods. These CNN-amplified hexagonal-grid artifacts do not affect Stokes parameters because they are orthogonal to polarization modes.The method delivers a statistically significant sensitivity gain for Her X-1, consistent with Cibrario et al. 2025 and equivalent to ~20% more effective area without extra observing time. These results support systematic use of hybrid reconstruction on IXPE astrophysical data.



Machine Learning Applications in the Solar Interior and Atmosphere (seen before)

Authors: Vishal Upendran, Carlos J. Díaz Baso
Categories: astro-ph.SR, astro-ph.IM, cs.LG
Comments: Review chapter submitted to the book Machine Learning Techniques for Astrophysics and Cosmology by Spring Singapore
Published: 2026-10-26
Matching Keywords: data-driven (abstract), multi-modal (abstract)

Abstract: The solar interior and atmosphere constitute a strongly coupled, multiscale dynamical system defined by continuous temporal evolution. Predicting the state of this system requires modeling physical interactions that span disparate spatial and temporal scales, constrained by heterogeneous and sparse data. This chapter formalizes the Sun within the mathematical framework of dynamical systems theory, defining a global state vector ($\vec{S}$) governed by thermodynamical, kinetic, and magnetic properties. Because solar observations represent projected, multi-modal, and partially observable measurements ($\vec{O}[\vec{S}]$) of this hidden state, pure physics-based deduction is fundamentally constrained. We present a unifying thesis: the primary role of machine learning in heliophysics is to act as a structured family of mathematical operators---specifically Inference ($\mathcal{I}$), Emulation ($\mathcal{E}$), Propagation ($\mathcal{P}$), and Control ($\mathcal{C}$)---that reconstruct, simulate, and couple this global state vector across disparate physical layers. By framing the vast, multi-disciplinary literature within this unified state-space taxonomy, we demonstrate how data-driven techniques bridge observational gaps to resolve hidden multiscale couplings and dramatically accelerate the simulation of non-linear processes, establishing a rigorous pathway to predict the global evolution of the Sun.
