Learning without relearning physical symmetry: an SO(3)-equivariant convolutional network (CNN) on the sphere

Authors: Joshua Williamson, Niall Jeffrey, Lorne Whiteway
Categories: stat.ML, astro-ph.CO, cs.LG
Comments: No comments
Published: 2026-10-16
Matching Keywords: convolutional (title, abstract), neural network (abstract)

Abstract: Many of the most important datasets in the physical sciences live natively on the sphere, and these data often carry no preferred position or orientation. Using methods that do not reflect this symmetry leads to far higher simulation requirements, which can be catastrophic for scientific analyses (forcing reliance on cheap, imperfect models that bias results). We present a novel spherical implementation of a gauge equivariant mesh architecture that is specifically designed for the Hierarchical Equal Area isoLatitude Pixelization (\healpix{}) of the sphere. The $\mathrm{SO}(3)$-equivariant convolution is built from steerable filters, parallel transport and spin-weighted features. The architecture supplies the exact symmetry, so the network does not spend training data learning it. The convolution operations are constructed to give provably exact equivariance, given a band-limit. Our full CNNs can achieve SO(3)-invariance error at per-cent-level and lower, which is controlled through well-understood hyperparameters. On benchmarks, our technique gives the most accurate dense gradient regression and competitive cosmic microwave background foreground removal and denoising. The improved simulation efficiency matters most in simulation-based inference (SBI), where high-fidelity simulations are expensive and neural compressors require large training sets, so a fixed budget forces analyses with less accurate simulators. In cosmology lensing SBI, our technique yields the tightest posteriors at matched simulation count. This approach is a novel method that will enable new convolutional neural network analyses, including generative and inference tasks, for processes and data on the sphere.



A Neural Network - Based Tool for Rapid Orbital Solution of Contact Binary Systems

Authors: Li-Heng Wang, Kai Li, Ya-Ni Guo
Categories: astro-ph.SR, astro-ph.IM
Comments: 13 pages, 8 figures, 3 tables, accepted by ApJ
Published: 2026-10-16
Matching Keywords: neural network (title, abstract), time-domain (abstract)

Abstract: With the rapid growth of large-scale time-domain surveys, vast amounts of photometric and spectroscopic data are continuously being released. A substantial number of contact binaries have been identified, accompanied by both light curves and radial velocity observations. Traditional modeling of these datasets relies on computationally intensive physical models, which limits their applicability to large-scale analyses. To this end, this paper proposes the use of a fully connected neural network and a SIREN (Sitzmann et al. 2020) model to rapidly generate radial velocity curves. After training, the models can rapidly generate radial velocity curves with high accuracy, significantly reducing computational costs and enabling efficient large-scale studies. This method was further integrated with the light curve computation framework developed by Li et al. (2025), allowing simultaneous fitting of radial velocities and light curves. To assess the reliability of the proposed model, synthetic observational datasets were constructed and used for parameter estimation. The deviations between the derived parameters and the ground-truth values were analyzed quantitatively, enabling a comprehensive evaluation of the model's accuracy.



Multi-modal transmission forecasting for the Vera C. Rubin Observatory

Authors: Peter Xiangyuan Ma, Joshua S. Bloom
Categories: astro-ph.IM
Comments: 54 pages, 7 figures, submitted to SPIE JATIS
Published: 2026-10-15
Matching Keywords: multimodal (abstract), multi-modal (title)

Abstract: In the age of large automated surveys conducted at facilities like the NSF-DOE Vera C. Rubin Observatory, delivering precision science goals demands highly efficient scheduling strategies that remain agile and dynamic to changing environments on the summit. On site, a variety of telemetry informs the scheduler of the best next pointing given the atmosphere's transmission. This live monitoring is provided by the Dutch Rubin Enhanced Atmospheric Monitor (DREAM) all-sky camera. Currently, dynamic scheduling uses short-term predictive algorithms, such as tracking cloud cross-correlations, to anticipate movement at short scheduling timescales. However, many of Rubin's core science objectives require survey strategies to commit to observations 20--40,min in advance (pair mode), which remains out of reach because clouds at those timescales can arrive from beyond the horizon. We developed a single monolithic multimodal strategy that folds asynchronous data from both the DREAM camera and weather satellite imagery to forecast multi-horizon sky transmission at 90,s, 5,min, 20,min, and 30,min in advance. We demonstrate that at 20-minute timescales, our model reduces transmission error by 21% and false-clear misses by 22% versus Rubin's frozen-flow tracker, and reduces false-clear misses by 26% versus persistence. We also demonstrate empirically, through ablations, that the DREAM all-sky camera provides short-horizon predictive power while satellite imagery provides long-horizon predictive power. We estimate the optimistic order-of-magnitude gain for survey efficiency improvement to be between 0.1% to 1%.



A New AI/ML Based Image Reconstruction Technique for Hard X-ray Coded Aperture Imagers

Authors: Shayla Sharmin, Branden Allen
Categories: astro-ph.IM, astro-ph.HE
Comments: No comments
Published: 2026-10-15
Matching Keywords: convolutional (abstract), neural network (abstract), attention (abstract)

Abstract: Here we demonstrate a new AI/ML based image reconstruction method for coded-aperture instruments that provides a pathway for embedded real-time use on-board spaceflight missions. Using the Swift Burst Alert Telescope (BAT) for our initial simulated demonstration, we used our new technique to replace the standard Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) based image reconstruction pipeline. In our alternative approach, we have created a neural network that maps detector counts directly to source positions using simulated responses from selected Swift Burst Alert Telescope (BAT) observations. The network combines convolutional feature extraction with attention and is trained at around 900 source counts. Under zero background noise, it achieves over 93\% exact-pixel localization accuracy with 400 detected source photons. The model maintains at least 90\% exact-pixel localization accuracy with up to 8{,}500 added background counts for sources with 900 detected photons. Within the evaluated region of BAT's field of view, these results show that source position can be recovered from sparse coded-aperture measurements in a wide-field instrument. A key feature of this technique is that explicit inclusion of the mask pattern is not required. This allows us to train the algorithm to "see" using data accumulated from an imaging system. These new non-traditional imaging techniques show substantial promise in enabling efficient imaging using non-planar geometries, which will enable new classes of hard X/$γ$-ray imaging systems and simultaneously improve imaging capability for currently operating instruments.



Low-cost neural summaries for cosmological inference: an ensemble of convolutional neural networks trained on approximate simulations

Authors: Iñigo Sáez-Casares, Davide Bianchi, Matteo Calabrese, Luigi Guzzo
Categories: astro-ph.CO
Comments: Submitted to A&A. 15 pages, 17 figures
Published: 2026-10-14
Matching Keywords: convolutional (title, abstract), neural network (title, abstract)

Abstract: Machine learning can build near-optimal summary statistics for extracting cosmological information from the large-scale structure of the Universe. Training them, however, requires large numbers of costly simulations. We aim to build a neural summary trained on fast, approximate low-resolution simulations that remains informative when applied to higher-resolution ones, reducing the cost of the training set. We train an ensemble of convolutional neural networks with different architectures to compress the matter overdensity fields of low-resolution particle-mesh simulations. The outputs of a subset of networks are concatenated into a summary statistic, which we use to constrain $Ω_{\rm m}$ and $σ_8$ from high-resolution particle-mesh simulations, each about a thousand times more costly than their low-resolution counterparts. Theoretical predictions are provided by a Gaussian process emulator built from the high-resolution simulations. We test three strategies for selecting the smallest informative subset of networks. Constraints are compared with those from a reference power spectrum analysis. We also consider dark matter halo catalogues from independent high-resolution $N$-body simulations. The neural summary extracts information beyond the binned power spectrum. Using multiple networks is essential: a single network can be less informative than the power spectrum. A greedy search based on posterior volume provides the tightest constraints with the fewest networks. The neural summary also outperforms the power spectrum for dark matter haloes, at different number densities and redshifts, despite being trained on low-resolution matter fields at $z=0$. This work demonstrates that an ensemble of networks trained on approximate simulations yields a summary statistic that remains informative on higher-fidelity data, including dark matter haloes. Identifying Planetary Nebulae Candidates from IPHAS and VPHAS+ Surveys via YOLO Deep Learning Models

Authors: Titouan Sérandour, Anne-Laure Méalier, Bruno Colicchio, Dominique Daniel, Philippe Gilberton, Philippe Oros, Thomas Boch, Djaffar Ould Abdeslam
Categories: astro-ph.IM, astro-ph.GA
Comments: 15 pages
Published: 2026-10-14
Matching Keywords: convolutional (abstract), transformer (abstract)

Abstract: We present a deep learning detection method capable of effectively identifying planetary nebulae (PNe) on IPHAS and VPHAS+ surveys. Our detection pipeline is based on the YOLO architecture and is adaptable to other datasets. Using the HiPS system, we preprocess and augment the $r$, $i$, and $Hα$ bands to produce two red, green, and blue (RGB) image datasets: (1) $640 \times 640$ px images at full resolution, and (2) $1 280 \times 1 280$ px images designed to capture extended objects up to $0.2$ deg. Images of $\sim 2600$ PNe are labelled using the HASH database. The loss function and evaluation metrics are adapted to the diffuse and low-contrast nature of PNe; a very low intersection over union (IoU) threshold ($1\%$) is used. A $k$-fold cross validation shows that the YOLOv12m model achieves an average precision of $0.979$ on the first dataset. At a confidence threshold of $0.15$, it achieves a precision rate of $98.7\%$ and a recall rate of $97.4\%$. We deployed our system (on $1280 \times 1280$ images) on the full surveys' coverage ($\sim 3755$ deg$^2$), and present a preliminary analysis of the detection results. The system is robust against artifacts such as CCD corner defects and HiPS tile joints. Our convolutional-neural-network-based model achieved excellent results on the test set, comparable to-or exceeding-that of a recent Swin Transformer approach. However, a challenge persists when applying deep learning models to extensive and densely populated Galactic fields: the development of an automated system that can effectively discriminate PNe from mimics that human experts are able to distinguish (e.g., H II regions). We outline possible improvements for future work.



Simulation and Deep Learning Inversion for Full-sky Energetic Neutral Atom Imaging at the Heliospheric Boundary

Authors: Weining Wang, Rong Lin, Jiansen He, Hong Zou, Yongfu Wang, Jiali Chen, Yuguang Ye, Weihong Shi, Wei Zhang, Xi Lu, Die Duan, Tianhang Chen, Linghua Wang, Qiugang Zong
Categories: astro-ph.IM
Comments: Submitted to Space: Science & Technology
Published: 2026-10-12
Matching Keywords: convolutional (abstract), neural network (abstract)

Abstract: The distribution of energetic neutral atom (ENA) differential flux density at the heliospheric boundary is the key to revealing the interaction mechanism between the solar wind and the interstellar medium. The Helios ENA Rapid Mapper (HERMA) is a pre-research project for a new ENA detection concept and technical principle, which is designed to provide technical support for refined detection with its advantages of wide energy range coverage and high spatial resolution. To verify the high spatiotemporal resolution rapid imaging capability of the HERMA, this study restored the particle response characteristics of the HERMA (spatial modulation grid + 256-pixel linear array silicon detector) based on Geant4 simulation, and we compared the performance of the traditional inversion algorithm with the convolutional neural network (CNN) inversion algorithm using INCA data as the benchmark. The results show that under different sampling times, the source distribution inverted by the CNN algorithm has a higher degree of agreement with the ideally expected distribution, and the CNN algorithm exhibits better stability and accuracy than the traditional algorithm under the conditions of low signal-to-noise ratio and short staring time. This study provides core technical support for the engineering verification and on-orbit application of the HERMA instrument, fills the technical gap in wide-energy-range and high-resolution ENA detection, and helps to deepen the research on the interaction mechanism between the solar wind and the interstellar medium at the heliospheric boundary.



Untangling Similarities and Differences in Star Formation Histories Across Cosmological Simulations (seen before)

Authors: Alicia M. Savelli, Matthew A. Kustec, Joshua S. Speagle, Kartheik G. Iyer, Eshal Ahmed, Zehao Peng, Norman Murray, Cissy Kuang
Categories: astro-ph.GA
Comments: 36 pages, 14 figures
Published: 2026-10-12
Matching Keywords: dimensionality reduction (abstract), neural network (abstract)

Abstract: How galaxies assemble depends on processes ranging from small-scale baryonic physics to large-scale cosmological assembly, which are difficult to disentangle with observations alone. Using $\sim$63,000 simulated galaxies from seven cosmological simulations spanning hydrodynamical, semi-analytic, and semi-empirical models, we explore how specific star formation histories (sSFHs) vary across modelling assumptions. Using non-linear dimensionality reduction (UMAP), we find that sSFH shape variation organizes along a few smooth axes in a 2D embedding, broadly corresponding to (1) peak timing, (2) peak sharpness, and (3) subsequent quenching rate. We identify sSFH shapes that many simulations share ("common"), well described to first order by dark matter halo assembly, and a few tied to individual simulations ("unique"), which likely reflect subgrid and parametrization choices such as merger-triggered burst prescriptions. A notable exception is a common population of rapidly quenched galaxies found across most simulations, likely driven by e.g., active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback, that halo assembly alone cannot reproduce. The origin of the most distinctive unique shape, an Illustris-dominated history with an early peak and slow decline, remains unclear, but we test the effects of short-term variability. With a multi-objective auto-encoder neural network, we recover the simulation that a given sSFH is drawn from with $\sim$95\% out-of-sample accuracy; ablation tests indicate this information is embedded in long-timescale sSFH morphology rather than short-term variability. These results provide avenues for theoretical and observational follow-up connecting star formation histories to subgrid physical models.



OASIS: On-the-fly ASynchronous Image Stream classification -- Transformer-based models for the early detection of strongly lensed supernovae

Authors: Satadru Bag, Raoul Canameras, Sherry H. Suyu, Stefan Schuldt, Stefan Taubenberger, Irham Taufik Andika, Alejandra Melo
Categories: astro-ph.IM
Comments: 17 pages, 9 figures, submitted to A&A
Published: 2026-10-12
Matching Keywords: time-domain (abstract), transformer (title, abstract), attention (abstract)

Abstract: Time-domain surveys often produce asynchronous image sequences across irregular observation times and photometric bands. In many applications, predictions are needed after each new observation, particularly when transient sources must be identified early to trigger follow-up. Strongly lensed supernovae (LSNe), for which follow-up is particularly time-critical, provide a useful example. We formulate this setting as On-the-fly ASynchronous Image Stream (OASIS) classification and explore reusable transformer architectures for causal, on-the-fly classification. We demonstrate these architectures on the lensed-transient classification problem studied by Bag et al. (2026a,b), distinguishing LSNe Ia from unlensed astrophysical transients. We compare them with the recurrent baseline adopted by Bag et al. (2026b), which combines a ConvLSTM branch for image processing with a separate LSTM branch for encoding observation times. The baseline maps asynchronous observations onto a fixed band-by-observation grid, filling unobserved bands with zeros. Using a common training setup, we compare two transformer-based approaches to modeling interactions among spatio-temporal tokens: factorized attention, which separates spatial and temporal interactions, and joint attention, which models them together. On the demonstration dataset, factorized architectures achieve mean losses of $0.095$--$0.100$, compared with $0.128$ for the recurrent baseline and $0.107$ or higher for joint architectures. A selective readout further reduces the best loss to $0.089$ for a factorized model. These results suggest that factorized attention suits this dataset better, without implying universal architectural superiority. We release the architectures as reusable foundations for time-domain astronomy and other applications involving evolving spatial features that require on-the-fly predictions.

Equation of State Classification of Core-Collapse Supernovae with a Network of Gravitational-Wave Detectors

Authors: A. Akhmetali, Y. S. Abylkairov, J. A. Font, M. Zanolin, E. Abdikamalov
Categories: astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.IM, gr-qc
Comments: Submitted to PRD
Published: 2026-10-12
Matching Keywords: convolutional (abstract), neural network (abstract)

Abstract: Gravitational waves from core-collapse supernovae may provide a unique probe of the equation of state (EOS) of dense nuclear matter. In previous work, we assessed the potential of machine-learning methods for EOS classification for rapidly rotating supernovae using individual gravitational-wave detectors. Here, we extend the framework to detector networks and investigate the impact of detector configuration, source distance, arrival time, and sky location on the classification. Using convolutional neural networks, we perform 10-class EOS classification directly from noisy detector data for both second- and third-generation observatories. We find that detector geometry strongly influences classification performance. For Galactic events, current detector networks can achieve promising classification performance, but the reach depends sensitively on the detector network antenna response. Adding detectors improves time-averaged performance and produces more uniform classification across the sky. Third-generation observatories extend the classification reach to 100 kpc for favorable source orientations but do not remove the dependence on detector geometry.



A Comprehensive JWST Slitless Spectroscopic Census of $\sim$12,000 Emission-Line Galaxies at Redshift $0\sim9$ in COSMOS

Authors: Shuqi Fu, Zijian Zhang, Danyang Jiang, Jie Chen, Linhua Jiang, Chenxi Bao, Yihe Wang, Ronaldo Laishram
Categories: astro-ph.GA
Comments: 24 pages, 11 figures, submitted to ApJS
Published: 2026-10-09
Matching Keywords: convolutional (abstract), neural network (abstract)

Abstract: Emission-line galaxies provide key diagnostics of star formation, chemical enrichment, and AGN activity across cosmic time. We present a systematic census of emission-line sources using JWST/NIRCam wide-field slitless spectroscopy (WFSS) from the COSMOS-3D program, which covers a large contiguous area of 0.33 deg$^2$ and provides F444W grism spectroscopy over $3.9$--$5.1\ μ$m. By combining slitless spectroscopy with deep multi-band imaging data, we construct a homogeneous spectroscopic sample of 12,275 objects at redshift 0--9. The typical $3σ$ line-flux limit reaches $3.2\times10^{-18}\ {\rm erg\ s^{-1}\ cm^{-2}}$. To address source confusion inherent to slitless spectroscopy, we develop a multi-step identification framework that combines information from spectra, images, spectral energy distributions, and spatial distributions. We introduce convolutional neural network and large language model to emulate the human identification process and improve efficiency. The resulting catalog provides spectroscopic redshifts, emission-line measurements, and derived physical properties for all sources. The F444W WFSS enables the detection of multiple emission lines, including primarily [O III], H$α$, He I $\lambda10830$, [S III] $λ\lambda9531,9069$, Pa$β$, Pa$α$, Br$β$, PAH, Br$α$, allowing a continuous census of galaxies across cosmic time. We construct composite spectra in several redshift bins and detect a variety of faint spectral features that are difficult to identify in individual spectra, including higher-order hydrogen recombination lines, CO absorption bands, and [Fe II] lines. This catalog provides a key reference dataset for studies of galaxy evolution and AGN activity, and demonstrates the power of WFSS for constructing large, unbiased spectroscopic samples over wide areas. The full catalog will be publicly available online upon publication.



Data Representation Matters: Optimizing Machine Learning for X-ray Source Classification by Leveraging Spatio-Spectral Information

Authors: Maggie Lieu, Ivan Valtchanov, Marguerite Pierre, Esin Gulbahar, Elias Koulouridis, Iván Muñoz Rodríguez, Lucio Chiappetti
Categories: astro-ph.IM
Comments: No comments
Published: 2026-10-08
Matching Keywords: convolutional (abstract), neural network (abstract)

Abstract: Distinguishing between faint extended X-ray sources is a fundamental yet challenging task in X-ray surveys. This paper presents a benchmark comparison of convolutional neural network models for classifying simulated XMM-Newton observations of AGN and galaxy clusters, focusing on the impact of data representation and interpretability of that data. We evaluate 1D, 2D, 2D+1D, and 3D CNNs trained on distinct data representations derived from simulated XMM-Newton observations of AGN and clusters: 1D (spectral), 2D (imaging) and 3D (event cube) data. We further apply 3D-GradCAM to interpret the learned features and assess the physical basis for the model's decision making to move beyond black-box classification. Our results demonstrate that increased spectral resolution enhances classifier performance using 1D and 2D information provides a strong baseline, the integration of both domains yields significant diagnostic gains. We find that a 3D CNN architecture applied directly to spatio-spectral event cubes provides the most robust performance, with superior consistency across cross-validation folds. Interpretability analysis reveals that the 3D model autonomously learns to distinguish the diffuse thermal emission of the intracluster medium from the localized, non-thermal power-law signatures of AGN. It exploits multi-dimensional correlations that are lost in lower-dimensional projections. While the performance gain over combined 2D and 1D baselines is marginal, the 3D approach demonstrates an ability to perform implicit background characterisation. By operating directly on total photon counts within event cubes, this method bypasses the need for manual background subtraction and modelling, offering a consistent, streamlined, end-to-end pipeline for automated classification of X-ray sources in large-scale catalogues.



ROSE (Red-giant Oscillations Spectra Estimator): A Modular Machine-Learning Framework for Automated Asteroseismic Characterisation. I. $ν_{\max}$ and $Δν$ from TESS (seen before)

Authors: Nipun Ghanghas, Dinil B. Palakkatharappil, Rafael A. Garcia, Shravan Hanasoge
Categories: astro-ph.SR, astro-ph.IM
Comments: 22 pages, 4 figures, 3 tables
Published: 2026-10-07
Matching Keywords: neural network (abstract), time-domain (abstract)

Abstract: Space-based photometry from Kepler and TESS has delivered oscillation spectra for hundreds of thousands of red giants, and PLATO and the Roman Galactic Bulge Time-Domain Survey will add more. We present ROSE, a modular machine-learning framework for automated asteroseismic characterisation of red giants, and apply two of its modules to the frequency of maximum oscillation power, $ν_{\rm max}$, and the large frequency separation, $Δν$. Each module is a neural network bundled with its own preprocessing transform and output binning, so that each parameter is measured in the representation that displays it most effectively: $ν_{\rm max}$ from the power-density spectrum, and $Δν$ from the power spectrum of the power spectrum taken over a window centred on $ν_{\rm max}$. Both are trained only on synthetic spectra generated at a one-year baseline and both return probability distributions rather than point estimates. This enables us to retrieve asymmetric uncertainty and a reliability index. Validating against held-out synthetic spectra, Kepler spectra at the training resolution and TESS continuous-viewing-zone giants, we obtain robust dispersions of 1.4, 3.3 and 4.4% in $ν_{\rm max}$ and 0.35, 0.61 and 1.3% in $Δν$, with the $ν_{\rm max}$ uncertainties close to their nominal coverage. The fully automated pipeline was applied to 274,721 TESS red-giant candidates observed for at least ten sectors, the framework returning reliable $ν_{\rm max}$ for 56,224 stars and reliable $Δν$ for 39,939, taking 8 ms per star. The sample follows $Δν= 0.278 ν_{\rm max}^{0.757}$ with a scatter of 6.2%, and the red-clump phase stands out as a distinct overdensity, although neither module is provided an evolutionary label or asked to produce one.



A Deep Learning Approach to Improving the Arrival-Direction Accuracy of Gamma-Ray Air-Shower Measurements with the Tibet-III Air-Shower Array

Authors: S. Okukawa, M. Kobayashi, K. Hara, K. Hibino, Y. Katayose, K. Kawata, M. Ohnishi, T. K. Sako, T. Sako, A. Shiomi, M. Takita
Categories: astro-ph.IM
Comments: Submitted to Experimental Astronomy
Published: 2026-10-07
Matching Keywords: convolutional (abstract), neural network (abstract)

Abstract: The Tibet AS$γ$ experiment observes cosmic gamma rays from several teraelectron volts to the petaelectron volt range using an array of ground-based surface air-shower detectors (Tibet-III), which is composed of plastic scintillators. The arrival direction is conventionally reconstructed by fitting the shower front to detector hit-timing data, and the resulting angular resolution is approximately 0.5$^\circ$ to 0.2$^\circ$ in the energy range from 10 to 100 TeV. In this study, to further improve the directional reconstruction accuracy, we developed a new arrival-direction reconstruction method that integrates a convolutional neural network (CNN) with the conventional method. Evaluations using gamma-ray events generated by Monte Carlo simulations show that the angular resolution is improved by approximately 10\,\%--15\,\% compared with that achieved with the conventional method. In addition, this performance improvement shows little dependence on the zenith angle up to 40$^\circ$. The proposed method provides a performance gain equivalent to increasing the Tibet-III array, which consists of approximately 600 detectors, by roughly 150 to 200 detectors.
